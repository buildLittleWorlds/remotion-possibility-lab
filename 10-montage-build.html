BUILDPAGE 10 / 17

Build your three-clip montage

Use one prompt, inspect its preview, then continue. Keep the same project conversation so each step adds to the version you have already checked.

If you have not started a project yet, read the setup conversation. You can replace the example topic after you understand the small working version.

Three files, already trimmed

Download Clip A, Clip B, and Clip C. Each is four seconds at 30 fps. Place them in public/media.

Credit: © 2008 Blender Foundation / www.bigbuckbunny.org · CC BY 3.0. These are trimmed, resized excerpts. Keep the credit with your example.

1 / Make a plain sequence

@Remotion Create FootageMontage at 1280 by 720, 30 fps, 12 seconds. Use public/media/bunny-a.mp4, bunny-b.mp4, and bunny-c.mp4. Play them consecutively in that order, four seconds each, muted. Give each clip its own named sequence in the source. Add a small A, B, or C label and this credit: “© 2008 Blender Foundation / www.bigbuckbunny.org · CC BY 3.0”.

Check: inspect the boundaries at 4 and 8 seconds.

2 / Compare a cut and an overlap

@Remotion Add a transition input: cut or fade. Cut keeps the current 12-second sequence. Fade overlaps each neighboring pair by 18 frames, using a crossfade. Calculate the resulting duration as 324 frames, or 10.8 seconds. Keep the first clip fully visible at the start, and keep the final clip through the ending. Show a simple edit timeline alongside the preview.

Check: seek into the overlap. You should see both neighboring excerpts contributing.

3 / Give the viewer a different order

@Remotion Embed FootageMontage in a Remotion Player interface. Add an order selector with ABC, CBA, and BAC, plus the cut/fade selector. Reuse the same three media files. Changing either input pauses and resets playback. Update the player duration to match the transition choice, and show each clip’s start time on the edit timeline.

Check: choose CBA with a fade. C should appear first and the duration should be 10.8 seconds.

Continue to the testing page before adding your own footage.
